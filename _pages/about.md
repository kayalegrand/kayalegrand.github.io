I study language in autism. The question that drives my research is: **_what is the nature of language development, language knowledge, and language processing in low-verbal autistic individuals, and what can this tell us about language in the mind?_**

I am a doctoral candidate in <a href="https://psychology.uconn.edu/phd/developmental-psychology/" style="color: gray; text-decoration: underline" target="_blank">Developmental Psychology</a> at the University of Connecticut, advised by <a href="https://psychology.uconn.edu/person/letitia-naigles/" style="color: gray; text-decoration: underline" target="_blank">Letty Naigles</a>. I have an MS in <a href="https://emerson.edu/academics/academic-departments/communication-sciences-and-disorders/graduate-programs" style="color: gray; text-decoration: underline" target="_blank">Communication Sciences and Disorders</a> from Emerson College and a BA in <a href="https://www.pomona.edu/academics/departments/linguistics-cognitive-science" style="color: gray; text-decoration: underline" target="_blank">Linguistics and Cognitive Science</a> from Pomona College. I synthesize perspectives from each of these fields in my work.

I am also a licensed speech-language pathologist (SLP), and I worked as an SLP at two residential autism schools in the northeast before starting my PhD.
