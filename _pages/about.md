I study language in autism. The question that drives my research is: what is the nature of the language system in low-verbal autistic individuals?

I am a doctoral candidate in <a href="[https://example.com/cv.pdf](https://psychology.uconn.edu/phd/developmental-psychology/)" target="_blank">Developmental Psychology</a> at the University of Connecticut, advised by <a href="https://psychology.uconn.edu/person/letitia-naigles/" target="_blank">Letty Naigles</a>. I have an MS in <a href="https://emerson.edu/academics/academic-departments/communication-sciences-and-disorders/graduate-programs" target="_blank">Communication Sciences and Disorders</a> from Emerson College and a BA in <a href="https://www.pomona.edu/academics/departments/linguistics-cognitive-science" target="_blank">Linguistics</a> from Pomona College. I am a licensed speech-language pathologist (SLP), and I worked as an SLP at two residential autism schools in the northeast before starting my PhD program. I synthesize perspectives from each of these fields (Developmental Psychology; Communication Sciences and Disorders; Linguistics) in my work.
